# Behavioral analysis

Define the observable behavior and the construct it is intended to measure. Naturalistic observation reveals repertoires, sequences, and ecological context; controlled tasks isolate selected influences. Choose the balance appropriate to the question and the available records.

When interpreting task performance, consider sensory and motor capacity, motivation, internal state, and prior experience where they can explain the result. A change in performance alone does not uniquely identify learning, memory, anxiety, or a specific circuit process.

Use quantitative behavior and task conditions to make the proposed comparison interpretable. Behavioral similarity in a disease model supports a particular modeled phenotype, not automatic correspondence to the full human disorder.
