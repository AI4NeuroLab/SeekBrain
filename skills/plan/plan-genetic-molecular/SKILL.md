# Genetic and molecular techniques

Choose evidence appropriate to the molecular question: expression and localization, loss or gain of function, rescue, or biochemical mechanism. Spatial and temporal control of a perturbation can distinguish developmental effects from a gene's action in a particular cell population or period.

For expression data, distinguish RNA abundance, protein abundance, localization, and demonstrated function. Single-cell RNA sequencing measures transcriptomes and supports classification; relate the sampled cells, measurement depth, and classification level to the intended comparison. Anatomical or physiological correspondence requires the relevant evidence. Genetic access through a marker or driver has its own specificity and coverage.

Use the supplied measurements to define a feasible analysis. Apply perturbation concepts to the strength of an explanation when intervention data are absent; propose new experiments only when they belong to the requested study.
