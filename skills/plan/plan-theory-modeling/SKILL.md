# Theory and modeling

Identify what the model should explain: membrane dynamics, stimulus encoding, decoding, a circuit computation, or learning from experience. Select a level of abstraction that the observations can constrain.

Make the consequential assumptions explicit and identify an observation that would distinguish the proposed explanation from alternatives. Simplified neurons or circuit architectures can reveal computational principles; assess which conclusions depend on those simplifications.

Encoding asks how stimuli are represented in activity; decoding asks what can be inferred from activity. Successful decoding does not by itself establish how biological downstream neurons read that activity. Distinguish supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement-learning objectives, and distinguish a useful data-analysis model from a demonstrated biological learning mechanism.
