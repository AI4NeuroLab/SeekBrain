To investigate the relationship between inter-regional connection strength and anatomical distance in the larval zebrafish brain, the brain was divided into 36 non-overlapping regions covering ~82% of the brain volume, which were split into left and right hemispheres for a total of 72 brain regions; the connectivity matrix was treated as undirected and self-connections were excluded, resulting in 2,556 unique brain region pairs (72 × 71 / 2); for each pair, normalized connection strength (calculated by summing neurite lengths from all neurons that connect them and normalizing by the total volume of the two regions) was plotted against the Euclidean distance between their centers of mass, with the center of mass coordinates for each brain region determined using 3D Slicer software; the dashed line represents an exponential decay fit (linear model on log-transformed data), and the Pearson correlation coefficient (R = -0.39) was calculated and displayed on the plot.
