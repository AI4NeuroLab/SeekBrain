A first step toward understanding the brain's sensorimotor transformations is identifying cells whose neural activity closely tracks the stimulus input. To do this, perform stimulus regression for the looming stimulation. The stimulus regressors are constructed by convolving binary stimulus vectors (extracting from the averaged stim trace) with an impulse kernel of GCaMP6 (a causal exponential-decay kernel, decay half-time = 0.4 s, peak delay = 0.08 s, duration = 8 s), separating right and left stimuli. Use the z-scored trial-averaged cell acitivity to conduct regression. To visualize these results, plot whole-brain stimulus tuning maps aligned to the zebrafish outline, showing the anatomical location of all cells with Pearson’s correlation coefficient r > 0.5 for right and left stimuli.
